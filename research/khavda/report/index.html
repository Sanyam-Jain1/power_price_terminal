The park, and who buys its power

Khavda sits on a vast stretch of salt desert near the Pakistan border. Adani Green Energy (AGEL) builds and owns almost all of it, through a family of project companies, plus some plants it built for its own group companies, Ambuja Cements and Adani Ports. The plants feed three Khavda pooling stations of the inter-state grid. In the Western Regional Power Committee's data two Adani aggregators speak for them: PS1, mostly solar, and PS3, with more wind. An aggregator schedules all its members' plants as one and settles their deviations together.

Deviation charges: almost free for a renewable seller

A discom or a coal plant that deviates from its schedule settles at the Normal Rate, the highest of the day-ahead and real-time prices, multiplied up when the grid frequency is low. A wind or solar seller settles at its own rate: its PPA tariff, or the price its exchange sales cleared at. Within the first band, under-injection is simply bought back at the price it was sold at, and over-injection is paid at it. Adani's aggregators, like most other WRPC renewable entities, are still settled on the older 2015-era tiers, apparently under court protection. The first band there works out to about — MW for PS1, more than its whole night-time output.

  • Behind a curtailed park a battery earns several times what a standalone battery is contracted at, because its charging energy is free and the evening price it sells at is the market's highest.
  • A Ministry of Power letter of 27 August 2026 lets curtailed renewable power charge a co-located battery and be sold to anyone, with no change to the PPA.
  • AGEL is already building this, with — MWh at Khavda. It sells the first battery output to its group company PTSL at a long fixed price (see the buyers table), below the evening exchange price, so much of the value goes to Adani Energy Solutions.
  • The case weakens in 2027: once Phase III arrives there is little spilled solar to soak up, and the battery charges from the grid at midday prices instead.
  • Evening prices hold only while the day-ahead market stays rationed at its cap. The MSEDCL study found the evening market's unmet bids run to many gigawatts, so a few more would not move it.

Method and gaps

  1. Block data. Every wind and solar seller's file from WRPC's weekly zips of all-member block data, 6 April to 18 October 2026, joined to IEX day-ahead, real-time and green day-ahead prices for each block.
  2. Settlement rate. WRPC's own rate for each block: the declared PPA tariff, or the weighted average of the seller's transactions. Deviation payable and receivable reconcile to it. It is used here as a price signal, not as booked revenue.
  3. Potential output. Each month PS1's output is fitted as a solar term (the average capacity factor of the PPA solar plants at the neighbouring KPS2 station) plus a wind term (the PPA wind plants around Bhuj), using only blocks where the solar peers are below —% of capacity, where PS1 is not held back. The fit is applied to the midday blocks; potential minus actual is the curtailment estimate. It is a straight-line fit on peers some tens of kilometres away. AGEL's own statement of capacity-factor points lost to curtailment suggests a figure about half as large; the true loss is probably between the two.
  4. Real deviation penalty. Net deviation money minus deviation energy times the seller's own rate in each block.
  5. Cost to others. On each block, under-injection times (Normal Rate minus the seller's rate), less the same on over-injection.
  6. Company facts. AGEL's investor decks, call transcripts and annual report; CARE's rating rationale; CERC orders; WRPC's monthly transmission deviation statements for grid access.
  • Who received each block, by buyer, is in WRLDC's scheduling portal, which cannot be reached from here.
  • AGEL reports generation, revenue and curtailment for the whole company, not Khavda alone.
  • Which project companies are still infirm, and when each PPA's supply starts, is not published.
  • The settlement regime for each plant is inferred from fits to WRPC's numbers, not from a WRPC note.
